Privacy Policy

Snakes in the Grass · last updated 11 October 2026

Snakes in the Grass is a game for Android made by Adam Plummer, a sole developer in the United Kingdom. This page explains what the game collects, why, who handles it, and how to have it deleted. In this policy, "I" and "me" mean Adam Plummer, who is responsible for your data (the "controller" under UK data protection law).

  • You don't make an account, and the game never asks for your name, email or contacts. Signing in with Google Play Games is optional and only backs up your farm.
  • Crash reports are sent so I can fix the game.
  • Play data is sent only if you say Yes when the game asks, and you can turn it off any time in Settings.
  • The leaderboard shows your player name, made from the game's own farm words, and your scores to other players.
  • Ads are shown only when you choose to watch one for a reward. They come from Google AdMob.
  • Purchases (Remove Ads and two characters) go through Google Play; the game never sees your payment details. Your data is never sold.

What the game collects, and why

Crash and error reports

If the game crashes, freezes or hits an error, a report is sent to Unity's Diagnostics service so I can find and fix the problem. A report contains:

  • what went wrong (the technical details of the crash or error) and the game's last few log messages;
  • the phone's make and model, its processor, graphics chip, memory and screen size, the Android version, and the phone's language;
  • an approximate location (country and region) worked out from your internet address;
  • a random ID for this installation of the game, and the game's version.

Why: to keep the game working. This is my legitimate interest in fixing faults, and the reports don't identify you by name.

The leaderboard and your public profile

When the game connects, it signs in anonymously with Unity's Authentication service. This creates a random player ID for this installation; there is no email or password. The game gives you a random two-word player name made from its own farm words (for example Muddy Badger), which is what other players see. You can change it under Profile > Account by picking other words, but names can't be typed, so a name never carries anything personal.

Each season the house survives is posted to the leaderboards with its score, profit, days played, the heirloom ranks and skulls it was played with, and the profile picture you chose. The game also publishes a public profile record, which other players can open from the board: seasons played and finished, your best season, your lifetime farm value and heirlooms, crops harvested, goods produced, snakes dealt with, the most skulls you have finished with, your chosen picture, and the layout of your best and latest finished seasons' farms (fields, pens, traps, lures and how much grass was cut), which other players' phones draw as pictures.

The game's Share button makes a picture of your best farm with your player name and those numbers, and hands it to Android's share menu. The game sends it nowhere itself: it goes only to the app and the people you choose, under that app's own terms.

Why: the leaderboard is a feature of the game you are playing, and it can't work without posting scores. Weekly boards reset every Monday; all-time boards keep your best score until you ask me to delete it.

Signing in with Google Play Games (optional)

If you use Google Play Games on your phone, the game can sign you in with it, so a new phone or a reinstall keeps the same name on the leaderboard and the same farm. When you sign in, Unity's Authentication service links your Play Games account to the game's random player ID and stores your Play Games player ID with it. The game also saves a private copy of your career (your heirlooms, homestead, skulls, milestones, record, profile picture and the characters you have earned) to Unity's Cloud Save, so it can be restored on another phone. Only you can read that copy; the public profile described above is unchanged. The game does not see your email address or your Google password; the sign-in itself is handled by Google under Google's privacy policy.

Why: to keep your progress when you change or reset your phone. This is based on your choice to sign in; if you never sign in, nothing in this section happens.

Play data (only if you say Yes)

The game asks once whether it can send how your seasons go. Only if you say Yes, it sends Unity Analytics a short summary when a season starts (its skulls, heirloom ranks and character), at the end of each day (day, cash, profit, farm value, grass mowed and left long, whether you slept, snakes, kills, bites, losses and the house's strength), and when the season ends (how it ended, the day reached, the score and heirlooms earned). Unity also records basic session information: when the game starts and how long it runs, the phone model and Android version, an approximate country from your internet address, and the installation's random ID.

Why: to see which days are too hard or too easy and balance the game. This is based on your consent. You can change your answer at any time in Settings > Share play data; turning it off stops anything more being sent.

Game settings from the server

As it starts, the game asks Unity's Remote Config service for any balance changes (for example, how fast the grass grows back). The request carries the installation's random ID and the game's version, and nothing comes back but numbers for the game.

Why: so I can fix the game's balance without everyone needing an update. This is my legitimate interest in running the game.

Ads (only when you choose to watch one)

The game offers an ad in return for a reward: an emergency loan when a season would end, bonus heirlooms when a season is complete, and a daily gift. An ad is only ever shown when you tap to watch one; there are no ads between days and nights or anywhere else in play. If you don't want to watch, nothing else in the game changes.

Ads are provided by Google AdMob. To choose, show and count ads, and to stop fraud, Google's ads software in the game collects:

  • your phone's advertising ID (a resettable ID that Android provides for ads);
  • your internet address and an approximate location (country and region) worked out from it;
  • the phone's make and model, Android version, language and screen size, and the game's name and version;
  • how you interact with the ads you watch (for example, whether an ad was watched to the end, so the reward can be given).

Because the game may be played by teenagers, it asks for non-personalised ads only for players in the UK, the European Economic Area and Switzerland, and for players in the United States (the game goes by the country of your phone's mobile network or SIM, or else your phone's region setting; it never reads your location). Non-personalised ads are chosen from things like the game itself and your approximate location, not from your interests or what you do in other apps. Elsewhere, Google may personalise ads using your advertising ID. On any Android phone you can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalised ads, in your phone's Settings under Google > Ads (or Privacy > Ads).

Where the law requires your consent for ads, Google's consent message asks for it inside the game, and the game's Settings offer a way to change your choice.

Google uses this data under its own privacy policy, as an independent controller. See how Google uses information from apps that use its services and Google's privacy policy.

Why: the ads pay for the game, which is free to play. Rewarded ads are your choice each time.

Purchases

Remove Ads and the two characters sold in the game are bought through Google Play, which takes the payment under its own terms; the game never sees your card or bank details. The game receives a receipt for each purchase (which item, and Google's order reference), checks it, and records what you own on your phone. Each time it starts, it asks Google Play what you own, so purchases come back after a reinstall. If you have said Yes to play data, Unity Analytics also records that a purchase was made.

Why: to give you what you paid for. This is necessary to perform the purchase you made.

Kept on your phone only

Your saves, settings, farm pictures and a play log stay on your phone and are not sent anywhere. Uninstalling the game deletes them.

Who handles your data

Crash reports, the leaderboard, play data and game settings go to Unity Technologies, which provides these services and processes the data on my behalf. Unity may store and process it in the United States and other countries, with safeguards for international transfers as described in Unity's privacy policy.

Ad data goes to Google, as described under Ads above. Google may store and process it in the United States and other countries.

I don't sell your data, and nothing the game collects for crash reports, the leaderboard or play data is used for advertising.

How long it is kept

  • Crash and error reports: for a limited period set by Unity, after which they are deleted.
  • Leaderboard scores: weekly boards reset every Monday; all-time scores and your public profile record stay until you ask me to delete them.
  • Your Play Games link and the private copy of your career: until you ask me to delete them.
  • Play data: for as long as it helps me balance the game, within Unity's own retention limits.
  • Ad data: kept by Google under Google's own retention periods; I don't receive it.

Children

Snakes in the Grass is made for players aged 13 and over and is not directed at children under 13. I don't knowingly collect data from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has used the game, contact me and I will delete what is held for their installation.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask for a copy of your data, ask for it to be corrected or deleted, object to how it is used, and withdraw consent for play data at any time (in Settings). For ads, you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's Settings, and change your ad consent in the game's Settings where it is offered. To delete your account and its data, use Profile > Account > Delete my account in the game, or see Delete your account. For any other request, email snakesinthegrass.game@outlook.com with your player name from the leaderboard, so I can find your records. I will reply within one month.

If you're unhappy with how your data is handled, you can complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Changes to this policy

If the game starts collecting anything new, for example when a new feature sends something it doesn't today, this page will be updated before that version is released, and the date at the top will change.
